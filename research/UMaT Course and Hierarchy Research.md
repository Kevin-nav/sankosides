# **Institutional Architecture and Academic Hierarchy of the University of Mines and Technology (UMaT), Tarkwa: A Comprehensive Institutional Analysis**

## **1\. Introduction: Historical Genesis and Statutory Mandate**

The University of Mines and Technology (UMaT), situated in the historic mining enclave of Tarkwa in the Western Region of Ghana, represents a critical node in the educational infrastructure of West Africa. Its evolution from a technical institute to a fully autonomous public university reflects the broader trajectory of Ghana's industrial development strategies and the increasing necessity for specialized human capital in the extractive industries. To understand the current academic hierarchy and course offerings of UMaT, it is essential to first contextualize its statutory origins and geographical significance.1

### **1.1 Historical Evolution and Legal Framework**

The genesis of UMaT dates back to 1952, with its establishment as the Tarkwa Technical Institute. This founding was driven by the colonial and early post-colonial government's recognition of the need for technical skills to support the burgeoning gold mining industry in the Tarkwa-Prestea belt. In 1961, reflecting a shift towards more specialized training, the institution was re-designated as the Tarkwa School of Mines. This change marked a pivotal moment, aligning the institution's curriculum directly with the manpower requirements of the state-owned and private mining corporations operating in the region.1

The institution's path to university status involved a significant period of affiliation with the Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST). In 1976, it became a faculty of KNUST, and later, in October 2001, it was elevated to the status of a University College, known as the Western University College of KNUST. This period of tutelage allowed the institution to mature its academic governance structures and curriculum standards. Finally, the transformative step occurred in November 2004, when the Parliament of Ghana passed Act 677, formally establishing the University of Mines and Technology as a fully fledged, autonomous public university. This legislative act empowered the university to award its own degrees, diplomas, and certificates, initiating a new era of academic expansion and infrastructural development.1

### **1.2 Geographical and Campus Context**

The university's location is not merely incidental but central to its pedagogical model. The main campus is situated approximately 2 kilometers south of the Tarkwa township, occupying a land area of approximately 1.39 square kilometers. The terrain is characteristically undulating, interspersed with verdant lawns and tropical flora, creating an environment conducive to academic contemplation.2 More importantly, Tarkwa is the heart of Ghana's mining industry, hosting major gold and manganese mining operations. This proximity allows for a unique integration of classroom theory and field practice, a synergy that defines the university's "field-ready" graduate philosophy. The campus infrastructure includes notable halls of residence such as the Chamber of Mines Hall, the Gold Refinery Hall, and the M.T. Kofi Hall (K.T. Hall), which provide not just accommodation but a localized community for the student body.2

The university has expanded its footprint beyond Tarkwa to include a satellite campus in Essikado, Sekondi-Takoradi, known as the School of Railways and Infrastructure Development (SRID), and a study center in Bibiani. These expansions are strategic responses to the national need for railway engineers and the upskilling of mining communities in other regions.3

## **2\. Governance and Administrative Hierarchy**

The academic delivery at UMaT is underpinned by a robust governance structure defined by Act 677\. This hierarchy ensures that academic programs align with national development goals and international standards.

### **2.1 The University Council**

The apex of the university's governance is the University Council, which holds the ultimate authority over the institution's finances, property, and strategic direction. The Council serves as the bridge between the university and the state, ensuring accountability and adherence to the university's mandate.5

* **Chancellor:** The ceremonial head of the university is His Excellency John Agyekum Kufuor, the former President of Ghana, whose stature lends significant diplomatic and political weight to the institution.1  
* **Chairperson:** The Council is chaired by Prof. Mrs. Goski Alabi, appointed by the President of Ghana in consultation with the Council of State. Her role is to lead the Council in setting policies that guide the university's management.5  
* **Membership Composition:** The Council is designed to be representative of various stakeholders. It includes the Vice Chancellor, representatives from the Ghana Chamber of Mines (reflecting the industry partnership), the Ghana Tertiary Education Commission (GTEC), the Conference of Heads of Assisted Secondary Schools (CHASS), and elected representatives from the university's convocation (both professorial and non-professorial), senior and junior staff, and the student representative council (SRC).5

### **2.2 Executive Management**

The day-to-day administration and academic leadership are vested in the Executive Management team, led by the Vice Chancellor.

* **The Vice Chancellor (VC):** Currently Prof. Richard Kwasi Amankwah, the VC is the Chief Executive Officer and the chief academic officer. He is responsible for the overall planning, direction, and management of the university. The VC chairs the Academic Board and is an ex-officio member of all statutory committees. Reporting directly to the VC are strategic units such as the Planning and Quality Assurance Unit and the Office of International Programmes, highlighting the focus on maintaining standards and fostering global partnerships.5  
* **The Pro Vice Chancellor:** This officer acts as the deputy to the VC, assisting in the management of the university and exercising powers delegated by the VC. The Pro VC often chairs key committees related to academic curriculum and staff welfare.5  
* **The Registrar:** As the Chief Administrative Officer and Secretary to the Council, the Registrar, currently Dr. Theophilus Andoh-Robertson, manages the non-academic administration, human resources, and legal affairs of the university.5  
* **The Finance Officer:** Mr. Jonathan Sakoe (Acting) oversees the financial health of the institution, managing the Treasury, Accounts, and Procurement units to ensure fiscal responsibility.5

## **3\. Academic Hierarchy: Faculties and Departments (Main Campus)**

The core academic machinery of UMaT is organized into Faculties and Schools. This hierarchical arrangement groups related disciplines to foster interdisciplinary research and resource sharing. Currently, the main campus in Tarkwa hosts five Faculties and one School, while a second School operates at the Essikado campus.

### **3.1 Faculty of Mining and Minerals Technology (FMMT)**

The Faculty of Mining and Minerals Technology is the flagship faculty of the university, embodying its primary mandate. It is recognized as a unique center of excellence in the West African sub-region for training high-level personnel for the minerals industry. Its mission is to produce world-class professionals in mining, technology, and related disciplines through a systems approach to engineering education.1

#### **3.1.1 Departmental Structure**

The faculty comprises two specialized departments:

* **Department of Mining Engineering:** This department focuses on the engineering principles involved in the extraction of minerals from the earth's crust, including mine design, drilling, blasting, and excavation technologies.  
* **Department of Minerals Engineering:** This department deals with the processing of mined ores to extract valuable minerals, covering metallurgy, thermodynamics, and separation processes.6

#### **3.1.2 Academic Programmes**

The Faculty offers a vertical progression of courses from undergraduate to doctoral levels.

**Undergraduate Programmes (BSc):**

* **BSc Mining Engineering:** A four-year programme designed to equip students with the technical skills to design and manage mining operations.  
* **BSc Minerals Engineering:** Focuses on the science and technology of processing minerals and materials.9

Postgraduate Programmes:  
The faculty runs robust postgraduate schemes aimed at research and advanced professional training:

* **MSc and MPhil in Mining Engineering:** These degrees offer specialized options including Rock Mechanics, Mine Planning and Design, Explosive and Blasting Technology, Mine Machinery and Mechanism, Mine Health, Safety and Environment, Mine Economics and Evaluation, and Mine Resources Management.11  
* **MSc and MPhil in Minerals Engineering:** Focused on advanced metallurgical processes.  
* **PhD Programmes:** Available in both Mining Engineering and Minerals Engineering for advanced research.  
* **Doctor of Engineering (D.Eng):** A professional doctorate aimed at senior industry practitioners in Mining and Minerals Engineering.11

### **3.2 Faculty of Engineering (FoE)**

## **4\. The School of Railways and Infrastructure Development (Essikado Campus)**

In a strategic expansion to support the national railway agenda, UMaT established the School of Railways and Infrastructure Development (SRID) in Essikado, Sekondi-Takoradi. This campus operates under a Memorandum of Understanding with the Ministry of Railways Development, aimed at revitalizing the railway sector through specialized education.18

### **4.1 Mandate and Curriculum Focus**

The SRID is unique in its curriculum design. While it offers standard engineering degrees, the context is heavily skewed towards railway infrastructure. A defining feature of this campus is the requirement for *all* students, regardless of their major, to take compulsory railway-specific courses. These include:

* Introduction to Railways Engineering.

## **6\. Comprehensive Course Hierarchy and Certificate Architecture**

Beyond the degree programmes, UMaT has a structured hierarchy of certificate programmes designed to provide technical competency in specific trade areas. These are critical for the university's engagement with the local community and industry.

### **6.1 Hierarchy of Academic Credentials**

The academic offerings at UMaT follow a clear hierarchy:

1. **Certificate:** Typically 1-year duration, often weekend-based, focusing on practical skills (e.g., Surveying, Drilling). Entry requires passes in core subjects or mature applicant status.  
2. **Diploma:** Typically 2-year duration, offering a higher level of technical training than certificates. Targeted at technicians.  
3. **Bachelor of Science (BSc):** 4-year professional degrees. These are the core offering, requiring strict WASSCE entry grades.  
4. **Postgraduate Diploma (PgD):** A bridge for those seeking to upgrade their qualifications before a Master's.  
5. **Master of Science (MSc) / Master of Philosophy (MPhil):** Advanced degrees, with MPhil being research-focused and MSc often being taught/professional.  
6. **Doctor of Philosophy (PhD):** The highest academic research degree.  
7. **Doctor of Engineering (D.Eng):** A professional doctorate for high-level industry executives.11

## **7\. Admissions Architecture and Fee Considerations**

The admissions process at UMaT is rigorous, reflecting its status as a premier science and technology institution. The requirements differ based on the applicant's background and the level of study.

### **7.1 Undergraduate Entry Requirements (BSc)**

The standard pathway for admission is via the West African Senior School Certificate Examination (WASSCE) or its predecessor, the SSSCE.

* **Core Requirements:** All applicants must possess credit passes (A1-C6 for WASSCE) in three core subjects: **English Language, Mathematics, and Integrated Science**.  
* **Elective Requirements:** Applicants must also have credit passes in three elective subjects relevant to their chosen programme.  
  * **General Engineering:** Physics, Mathematics, and Chemistry are the standard electives.  
  * **Mechanical Engineering:** Accepts Technical Drawing or Metal Work in place of Chemistry.  
  * **Electrical/Computer Engineering:** Accepts Applied Electricity, Electronics, or ICT in place of Chemistry.  
  * **Geomatic Engineering:** Accepts Geography in place of Chemistry.3  
  * **FIMS/FCaMS Programs:** These faculties have broader entry criteria, accepting electives from General Arts (Economics, Geography, Government) and Business (Accounting, Costing) streams, depending on the specific degree (e.g., Logistics or Mathematics).10

Mature Applicants:  
The university provides an alternative pathway for mature students who are at least 25 years old. These applicants must hold five credits at GCE 'O' Level or WASSCE (including English and Math) and possess relevant working experience. They are subject to an entrance examination and/or interview to assess their suitability.24  
International Applicants:  
International students are welcomed and assessed on equivalent qualifications to the WASSCE/GCE 'A' Levels. The application process for them differs, involving a US$100 application fee which is added to their first-year tuition. They must submit scanned documents via email for assessment before receiving an offer.25

### **7.2 Postgraduate Admissions**

* **MSc/MPhil:** Requires a good first degree (Second Class Lower or better) in a relevant discipline. Industry professionals with a UMaT Diploma and significant experience (5+ years) may also be considered.  
* **PhD:** Generally requires a research-based Master’s degree (MPhil). Candidates with a non-research Master’s may be admitted to the MPhil programme first and upgraded to PhD status upon demonstrating sufficient research caliber.  
* **D.Eng:** This professional doctorate strictly requires a Master's degree and at least five years of high-level industrial experience.11

### **7.3 Fee Structure Insights**

While specific current fee amounts change annually, the research highlights a "Fee-paying" category for students who meet the minimum entry requirements (Aggregate 36\) but miss the competitive departmental cut-off points. Programmes like BSc Petroleum Engineering and Diploma in General Drilling are explicitly listed as fee-paying or having fee-paying options, allowing the university to generate internal revenue while expanding access.26 The fee for fee-paying students is significantly higher than for regular students, often double or triple the regular rate (e.g., GHS 6,282 vs GHS 1,843 in past schedules).26

## **8\. Strategic Insights: Research, Infrastructure, and Future Outlook**

The data suggests UMaT is in a phase of aggressive strategic diversification.

### **8.1 From Extraction to Digitalization**

The most significant trend is the shift from purely extractive disciplines to digital and management sciences. The establishment of the **Faculty of Computing and Mathematical Sciences (FCaMS)** and the introduction of degrees in **Robotics, AI, and Cyber Security** indicate a clear response to Industry 4.0. The partnership with the Ghana Chamber of Mines to build a **US$1.2 million AI and Robotics Centre** is a tangible manifestation of this strategy. This center will feature laboratories for Robot Navigation and Perception, positioning UMaT as a leader in "Smart Mining" technologies.15

### **8.2 Response to National Challenges**

The university is actively positioning itself as a solution provider for Ghana's illegal mining (Galamsey) crisis. The curriculum in the **Faculty of Geosciences**, particularly the **Department of Environmental and Safety Engineering**, along with research initiatives using AI to monitor galamsey operations, demonstrates the university's social relevance. The **Small Scale Mine and Quarry Administration** certificate is another direct intervention to formalize and professionalize the artisanal mining sector.15

### **8.3 Infrastructural Expansion**

The development of the Essikado campus into the **School of Railways and Infrastructure Development** aligns UMaT with the national government's railway master plan. By making rail courses compulsory, the university is ensuring a steady pipeline of specialized engineers for the sector. Furthermore, the **School of Petroleum Studies**, with its GNPC-funded infrastructure, secures the university's role in the oil and gas sector, reducing reliance on the traditional mining sector.17

## **9\. Conclusion**

The University of Mines and Technology has successfully transformed from a mono-technic institute into a comprehensive, multi-campus university. Its academic hierarchy is meticulously structured to serve the full spectrum of industrial needs, from the artisan level (via certificates at Bibiani) to the executive level (via the D.Eng programme). The main campus in Tarkwa remains the hub for deep research and core engineering, while the Essikado campus pioneers infrastructure education. With a governance structure anchored in statutory law and a curriculum that evolves with technological trends (AI, Robotics), UMaT is poised to remain the premier institution for minerals and technology education in West Africa.
