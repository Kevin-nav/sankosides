### **1. The Referencing Style (Crucial for Slides)**

The guide explicitly states that UMaT adopts the **Harvard System**. You must use this style on your slides for any data, quote, or image taken from another source.

#### **How to use it in PowerPoint:**

* **On the Slide (In-text Citation):** When you put a bullet point or image on a slide that comes from a source, you must cite the author and year immediately.
* **Last Slide (Reference List):** You must have a slide at the very end titled "References" listing the full details of the citations used in the slides.

#### **Detailed Rules & Examples (Harvard Style):**

**A. Single Author**
* 
**Rule:** Surname followed by Year.
* 
**Example:** (Cobblah, 1997).

**B. Two Authors**
* **Rule:** Both surnames connected by "and".
* 
**Example:** (Mireku-Gyimah and Suglo, 1993).

**C. More than Three Authors**
* 
**Rule:** Use the first author's surname followed by "et al.".
* 
**Example:** (Temeng et al., 2000).

**D. Multiple Articles by Same Author in Same Year**
* 
**Rule:** Add letters (a, b, c) to the year.
* 
**Example:** (Amegbey, 1990a).


**E. Organization/Webpage (Unknown Author)**

* **Rule:** Use "Anon." (Anonymous) .
* 
**Example:** (Anon., 2007).

### **2. Presentation Structure**

Your slide deck should follow the logical progression outlined for the thesis structure.

| Slide Section | Equivalent Thesis Chapter | Content to Include |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Title Slide** | **Title Page** | Thesis Title, Your Name, Degree Statement ("Submitted in fulfillment..."), Supervisor's Name, Date .

 |
| **Introduction** | **Chapter 1** | Problem Definition, Objectives, Methods Used (Briefly).

 |
| **Literature Review** | **Chapter 2** | Brief summary of previous work and how your work fills a gap. Do not just list authors; show the "state of the problem".

 |
| **Methodology** | **Chapter 3** | Source of data, experimental procedures, or facilities employed.

 |
| **Results & Discussion** | **Central Chapters** | Your specific contribution to knowledge. Use graphs here (see section 3 below).

 |
| **Conclusions** | **Final Chapter** | Summary of findings and specific recommendations.

 |
| **References** | **Reference List** | Alphabetical list of sources cited on the slides.

 |

### **3. Visuals (Tables & Figures)**

The guide provides strict rules for visuals which you should replicate on your slides for a professional look.

* **Numbering:**
* 
**Figures:** Label them as "Figure X" (e.g., Figure 2.1) and place the title **BELOW** the image.


* 
**Tables:** Label them as "Table X" (e.g., Table 2.1) and place the title **ABOVE** the table.




* **Clarity:**
* Do not clutter slides with complex working drawings; use simple diagrams that clarify the verbal content.


* If using photos, ensure they have a scale to show size.




* **Graphs:**
* Ensure units are clearly stated on axes.


* Use scales that are multiples of 10 for readability.

### **4. Formatting & Style (Transferable Rules)**

While these rules are for the document, adhering to them in PPT shows attention to detail:

* 
**Font Choice:** Use clear fonts like **Times New Roman, Arial, or Tahoma**. Avoid unusual scripts unless for emphasis.


* 
**Spelling:** Use **British English** (e.g., "colour" not "color").


* **Units:** strictly use **SI Units** (e.g., kg, m, s).
* Leave a space between the number and unit (e.g., `80 cm`, not `80cm`).


* Do not put a full stop after a unit (e.g., `kg` not `kg.`).


* Temperature: `20 °C` (space between number and degree, no space between degree and C).




* 
**Acronyms:** Spell out an acronym the first time you use it on a slide, then use the abbreviation (e.g., "University of Mines and Technology (UMaT)").
